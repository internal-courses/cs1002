Act I: The Wrong Metric

Marks Are a Lagging Indicator

For years, we identified "at-risk" students by their exam scores. It makes intuitive sense: if a student scores 0 on a question, they need help.

But a score of 0 hides everything that matters. Did they stare at the screen for 30 minutes? Did they write 50 lines of code that just happened to have a syntax error? Did they run the code 40 times, debugging furiously, but miss one edge case?

We found that process traces—the invisible logs of runs, edits, and errors—tell a different story. By ignoring the final score and looking only at how they struggled, we found a hidden cohort of 1,311 students who are failing, but are primed to learn.

The Full Cohort

We start with 13,761 active students. These are students who attempted the recent exams. If we used scores, 8,000 of them would look "at risk." But we can't coach 8,000 students at once.

Filter 1: The Stable (D2)

First, we remove the 7,869 students (57%) who are actually fine. They might have missed a question, but their process is healthy. They edit, run, and fix errors efficiently. They don't need intervention.

Filter 2: The Ghosts (D0)

Next, we identify the 911 students (6.6%) who barely engaged. They are "One-Shotters"—they pasted code once, failed, and quit. Or they wrote nothing. Coaching on syntax won't help them; they need re-engagement campaigns.

Filter 3: The Chaos (D1)

Then there are the 992 students (7.2%) in deep distress. Their logs show "thrashing"—rapid, random edits that make the code worse. They are stuck in loops of confusion. They need high-touch, human diagnostic triage, not standard tutorials.

Filter 4: The Mixed Bag (D3)

We set aside 2,678 students (19.5%) with ambiguous patterns. They struggle a bit, but also succeed a bit. They are the "watch list."

The Prize: The Teachable 1,311

This leaves us with exactly 1,311 students (9.5%). These are the goldilocks zone. They are trying hard (high runs/edits). They aren't thrashing. But they are stuck on specific, fixable barriers. We can help them now.

The Three Tracks

We don't just dump them in a bucket. We split them into three distinct intervention tracks based on their dominant error profile: Syntax, Runtime, or Logic.
